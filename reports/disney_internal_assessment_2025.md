# Disney internal assessment — 2025

## Executive summary
Our internal view of Disney improved steadily through 2025, with the team maintaining an Outperform rating while increasing conviction each quarter. The investment thesis evolved from an "early DTC profit inflection" to confidence in sustained DTC profitability accompanied by EPS growth and greater capital returns. Key observable drivers were repeated EPS guidance raises, rising DTC operating income, resilient Experiences results, and accelerated buybacks. Remaining cautions consistently included capex pressure from Parks & Cruises, sports rights inflation, content/box-office volatility, and streaming churn.

## Original research question / hypothesis
Can Disney achieve a sustained DTC profitability inflection that drives near‑term EPS recovery and supports durable capital returns (buybacks) while managing macro, content, and capex risks?

## Key findings
- Q1 FY25 Earnings Review (internal file: `disney_q1_fy25_research_note.pdf`): Internal stance was Outperform with PT $115. The note shifted the thesis to modestly more constructive, highlighting an early DTC profit inflection, a studio rebound, and durable Experiences demand; primary cautions were FCF pressure from parks/cruise capex, streaming churn, sports-rights inflation, and theatrical volatility. FY25E EPS cited at $5.39 and FY26E at $5.99. (Q1 FY25 Earnings Review; internal file: `disney_q1_fy25_research_note.pdf`)

- Q2 FY25 Earnings Review (post-Q2 results; publication date not specified): Rating remained Outperform; PT was raised to $120 after management raised FY25 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.75 and DTC operating income improved to about $336mm. The team increased conviction that DTC profitability was becoming real and repeatable, while noting risks including macro sensitivity, sports costs, streaming ARPU/churn, and elevated capex. (Q2 FY25 Earnings Review; post-Q2 results)

- Aug. 6, 2025 — Q3 FY25 Earnings Review: Outperform maintained and PT raised to $125. DTC operating income rose to roughly $346mm, Experiences stayed strong, and management raised FY25 EPS guidance to $5.85. The note increased confidence in Disney’s EPS pathway while flagging content volatility, ESPN rights costs, macro risk, and capex as remaining concerns. (Aug. 6, 2025 — Q3 FY25 Earnings Review)

- Nov. 13, 2025 — Q4 FY25 Earnings Review: Outperform maintained and PT raised to $135. The assessment characterized FY25 as proof the turnaround was taking hold: DTC profitable at scale, Experiences delivering record profit, and buybacks accelerating. FY25 adjusted EPS was about $5.93, and FY27E EPS around $7.10 was used to support the higher PT. Key risks remained slate volatility, linear ad pressure, sports inflation, Experiences cyclicality, and capex/tax timing. (Nov. 13, 2025 — Q4 FY25 Earnings Review)

## Bottom line
- Internal conviction improved across 2025 while maintaining an Outperform rating each quarter; price targets progressed sequentially from $115 → $120 → $125 → $135. (Consolidated findings table)
- The primary drivers of the improving assessment were: repeated EPS guidance raises; scaling DTC profitability; resilient Experiences (parks/cruise) performance; and stronger capital returns via buybacks. The team continued to call out content volatility, sports-rights inflation, macro sensitivity, and elevated capex as persistent risks. (Consolidated findings — Bottom line)
